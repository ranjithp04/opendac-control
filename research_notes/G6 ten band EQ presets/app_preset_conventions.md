# EQ preset conventions in established players and EQ apps (10-band graphic EQ)

Scope note: numbers below are *summarised shapes* (rounded, per band) taken from the open-source preset tables I could read directly (VLC, Webamp/Winamp, Cog, eqMac, AOSP). Full tables are not reproduced. Target grid for the later design step: 31, 62, 125, 250, 500, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 16k Hz, ±12 dB.

Band-grid caveat that affects every comparison: Winamp and VLC presets were authored on the Winamp grid **60, 170, 310, 600, 1k, 3k, 6k, 12k, 14k, 16k Hz**, not the ISO octave grid. VLC's header defines both tables (`f_vlc_frequency_table_10b` = Winamp grid; `f_iso_frequency_table_10b` = 31.25…16k) — [VLC equalizer_presets.h](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h). Apple-style presets (Cog, eqMac) use the octave grid 32, 64, 125/128, 250/256, 500/512, 1k, 2k, 4k, 8k, 16k — [Cog Equalizer.swift](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/iOS/App/Equalizer.swift). So Winamp's "12k/14k/16k" are three adjacent top bands; mapping them to the octave grid collapses them into 8k/16k.

## Q1. Which preset names recur across apps?

### Takeaway
Two families dominate: the **Winamp/VLC family** (Classical, Club, Dance, Full Bass, Full Treble, Full Bass & Treble, Headphones/Laptop speakers, Large Hall, Live, Party, Pop, Reggae, Rock, Ska, Soft, Soft Rock, Techno) and the **Apple/iTunes family** (Acoustic, Bass Booster/Reducer, Classical, Dance, Deep, Electronic, Hip-Hop, Jazz, Late Night, Latin, Loudness, Lounge, Piano, Pop, R&B, Rock, Small Speakers, Spoken Word, Treble Booster/Reducer, Vocal Booster), which is cloned by eqMac and Cog. Android's AOSP default is a small 5-band genre set. The names common to essentially all families: **Flat, Classical, Dance, Pop, Rock**; Jazz and Hip-Hop are common outside Winamp/VLC.

### Cited Findings
- VLC ships 18 presets: flat, classical, club, dance, fullbass, fullbasstreble, fulltreble, headphones, largehall, live, party, pop, reggae, rock, ska, soft, softrock, techno; each stored with a preamp value and 10 gains — [VLC equalizer_presets.h](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h)
- Webamp's built-in "Winamp EQ library file v1.1" (replica of Winamp's built-in winamp.q1 set) has the same 17 non-flat names, with "Laptop speakers/headphones" where VLC has "headphones" — [Webamp builtin.json](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); `.q1`/`.eqf` format and 1–64 slider scale — [winamp-eqf README](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/winamp-eqf/README.md)
- VLC's values are the Winamp set rescaled (~1.6× Winamp's dB, so VLC "Full Treble" reaches +16.8 dB, beyond ±12) — inference from comparing [VLC](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h) with [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json). One clear divergence: VLC `fullbass` has **−8 dB at 60 Hz**, whereas Winamp's Full Bass has ~+6 dB there (looks like a data error in VLC).
- iTunes/Apple Music has "more than 20 presets" plus a Preamp slider on the far left; users can "Make Preset" — [Apple support, iTunes for Windows](https://support.apple.com/guide/itunes/itns2991/windows)
- Macworld lists Apple presets incl. Classical, Jazz, Rock, Bass Booster/Reducer, Treble Booster/Reducer — [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3074418/streaming-media/how-to-tweak-your-sound-in-itunes-and-on-ios-devices.html) (search snippet only; not fetched)
- iTunes COM API documents per-band range −12 to +12 dB — [iTunes COM IITEQPreset](https://documentation.help/iTunesCOM/interfaceIITEQPreset.html) (search snippet only)
- Cog (open-source macOS/iOS player) ships `Cog.q1.json` with 22 presets whose names match Apple's list **except Late Night** (Acoustic, Bass Booster, Bass Reducer, Classical, Dance, Deep, Electronic, Flat, Hip-Hop, Jazz, Latin, Loudness, Lounge, Piano, Pop, R&B, Rock, Small Speakers, Spoken Word, Treble Booster, Treble Reducer, Vocal Booster); stored as Winamp-style 1…401 integers = −20…+20 dB in 0.1 dB steps, on 32…16k octave bands — [Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json), [Cog Equalizer.swift](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/iOS/App/Equalizer.swift)
- eqMac's Advanced EQ default presets use the same Apple names (Flat, Acoustic, Bass Booster, Bass Reducer, "Classic", Dance, Deep, Electronic, Hip-Hop, Jazz, Latin, Loudness, Lounge, Piano, Pop, "RnB", Rock, Small Speakers, Spoken Word, Treble Booster, Treble Reducer, Vocal Booster); no Late Night — [eqMac AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- Android AOSP (LVM effect bundle, the stock system Equalizer that many apps incl. Spotify-on-Android hand off to) has 10 presets: Normal, Classical, Dance, Flat, Folk, Heavy Metal, Hip Hop, Jazz, Pop, Rock — on **5 bands** (60, 230, 910, 3600, 14000 Hz) — [AOSP EffectBundle.h](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h)
- Spotify: sources conflict. Reported lists include (a) Normal/Pop/Classic/Jazz/Rock, (b) the AOSP list, (c) an Apple-style 22-preset list (Acoustic … Vocal booster); Android Spotify is reported to defer to the phone's system EQ — [freeyourmusic](https://freeyourmusic.com/blog/best-spotify-equalizer-settings), [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/the-best-spotify-equalizer-settings/), [Headphonesty](https://www.headphonesty.com/2022/03/best-spotify-equalizer-settings/), [musconv](https://musconv.com/spotify-equalizer-for-android/) (all search snippets; not individually fetched)
- WiiM (streamer) ships 24 presets that also follow the Apple naming (describes Treble Reducer as cutting 1 kHz–20 kHz, Vocal Booster raising 250 Hz–4 kHz, Lounge emphasising midrange) — [WiiM help center](https://faq.wiimhome.com/en/support/solutions/articles/72000594355-introducing-24-preset-eq-settings-for-ultimate-audio-performance) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- The Apple preset list has become a de-facto industry vocabulary (eqMac, Cog, WiiM, some Spotify builds all reuse it). Users will recognise those names.
- Use-case names (Bass/Treble Booster/Reducer, Small Speakers, Spoken Word, Vocal Booster, Loudness, Late Night, Headphones) are more portable across content than genre names.

### Gaps
- Samsung (SoundAlive/One UI), Poweramp and foobar2000 built-in preset name lists: not found in reliable sources within budget. foobar2000's stock 18-band EQ is believed to ship without genre presets, but I did not verify this.
- Equalizer APO itself ships no genre preset set that I could confirm; Peace ships some presets (users call them "way too hot") but I did not retrieve their names — [Peace SourceForge](https://www.sourceforge.net/p/peace-equalizer-apo-extension/wiki) (snippet).

## Q2. Typical per-band shapes and maximum boosts for the useful presets

### Takeaway
Apple-family presets are **gentle** (peak boosts ≈ +4…+6.9 dB, mostly smooth tilts/shelves with ~1–1.5 dB steps per octave). Winamp-family presets are **aggressive** (≈ ±5–10 dB in Winamp terms; VLC's rescaled versions go to +16.8 dB). eqMac's versions are roughly **2× the Apple-family values** (Bass Booster +11 dB). AOSP's shipped "soft" 5-band presets are modest (≤ +9 dB, mostly +3…+5).

All values below: Apple family = Cog.q1.json values (rounded to 0.5 dB); Winamp = Webamp builtin.json converted with (v−33)×12/31 (**approximation**, rounded to 1 dB, Winamp grid). Sources: [Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json), [eqMac presets](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift), [Webamp builtin.json](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json) with ±12 dB / 50%=0 dB scale per [Webamp media/index.ts](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/js/media/index.ts), [VLC presets](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h), [AOSP EffectBundle.h](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h).

### Cited Findings — per preset
- **Bass Booster** (Apple family): low shelf-like ramp: max **+5.5 dB at 32 Hz**, stepping ≈ +4.3/+3.5/+2.5/+1.3 through 64–500 Hz, 0 from 1 kHz up. eqMac: identical shape doubled (+11 → +2.5, 0 above 1 kHz). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- **Full Bass** (Winamp): ≈ +6 dB at 60/170/310 Hz, +3 at 600, ~+1 at 1k, then *cuts* the top: −3 (3k), −6 (6k), −7 (12–16k) ≈ approximation. VLC version: +9.6 at 170/310 Hz, down to −11.2 dB at top (with the anomalous −8 at 60 Hz). Winamp's "bass" is a tilt, not just a shelf. — [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [VLC](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h)
- **Bass Reducer**: exact mirror of Bass Booster (−5.5 at 32 Hz tapering to 0 by 1 kHz); eqMac −11. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- **Treble Booster**: mirror-image high ramp: 0 to 500 Hz, then ≈ +1.3 (1k), +2.3 (2k), +3.5 (4k), +4.3 (8k), **+5.5 (16k)**; eqMac doubled (+11 at 16k). **Treble Reducer** = exact negative. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- **Full Treble** (Winamp): ≈ −7 dB 60–310 Hz, −3 at 600, +2 at 1k, +7 at 3k, ≈ +10 at 6–16k (approximation); VLC: −9.6 → +16.8 dB. — [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [VLC](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h)
- **Full Bass & Treble / Party** (Winamp V/"smile"): Full Bass & Treble ≈ +4/+3 (60/170), 0 (310), −5 (600), −3 (1k), +1 (3k), +5 (6k), +7 (12–16k). Party is simpler: ≈ +4 at the two lowest and two highest bands, flat mids. — [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json)
- **Small Speakers** (Apple family): bass-up / treble-down tilt pivoting at 1 kHz: +5.5 (32) → +1.3 (500), 0 (1k), then −1.3, −2.5, −3.5, **−4.3 (16k)**; eqMac doubled (+11 / −8.5). Note: it boosts 32–64 Hz, which most small speakers cannot reproduce. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- **Loudness** (Apple family): +6 (32), +4 (64), 0 (125–250), −2 (500), 0 (1k), −1 (2k), **−5 (4k)**, **+5 (8k)**, +1 (16k). Unusual: a deep 4 kHz dip next to an 8 kHz boost, not a textbook equal-loudness curve. eqMac doubles it (+12 / −10 / +10). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift). Classic hi-fi "loudness" buttons were essentially a smiley curve approximating equal-loudness contours for quiet listening — [Wikipedia: Smiley face curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiley_face_curve) (snippet)
- **Late Night** (Apple): no open table found. Consumer blogs describe it as dynamic-range compression (lifting quiet parts) rather than a frequency curve — [Marquee.fm](https://marquee.fm/guides/apple-music-equalizer/), [Headphonesty](https://www.headphonesty.com/2022/07/iphone-equalizer-settings/) (snippets; **unverified**, possibly speculative).
- **Spoken Word** (Apple family): cut lows (−3.5 at 32, ~−0.5 at 64), ~0 at 125–250, broad lift **+3.5 (500), +4.6 (1k), +4.8 (2k), +4.3 (4k)**, +2.5 (8k), 0 (16k). eqMac ≈ 2× (up to +9.7 at 2k). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- **Vocal Booster** (Apple family): −1.5 (32), −3 (64/125), +1.5 (250), **+3.8 (500/1k)**, +3 (2k), +1.5 (4k), 0 (8k), −1.5 (16k) — a midrange hump, cut lows and extreme highs. eqMac 2× (+7.5 peak). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- **Rock**: Apple family is a mild smile: +5 (32), +4, +3, +1.5, −0.5 (500), −1 (1k), +0.5, +2.5, +3.5, **+4.5 (16k)**. Winamp's Rock is a deep V: ≈ +5 (60), +3 (170), −4 (310), **−5 (600)**, −3 (1k), +2 (3k), +5 (6k), +7 (12–16k) (approx.); VLC up to +11.2. AOSP Rock (5-band soft): +5, +3, −1, +3, +5 dB. All agree on "boost both ends, dip low-mids/mids"; disagree on depth (Apple −1 dB vs Winamp −5 dB). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h)
- **Pop**: universally an inverted smile (mid hump). Apple family: −1.5 (32), −1 (64), 0 (125), +2 (250), **+4 (500/1k)**, +2 (2k), 0, −1, −1.5. Winamp: ≈ −2 (60), +3 (170), +4 (310), **+5 (600)**, +3 (1k), −1, −2… (hump centred lower, ~300–600 Hz). AOSP Pop: −1, +2, +5 (910 Hz), +1, −2. Strong agreement on shape; peak ~+4–5 dB. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h)
- **Classical**: implementations disagree completely. Apple family: smile — +4.8 (32), +3.8, +3, +2.5, −1.5 (500/1k), 0 (2k), +2.3, +3.3, +3.8 (16k). Winamp: flat to 3k, then **cuts treble** ≈ −5 (6–14k), −7 (16k). AOSP Classical: +5, +3, −2, +4, +4 (smile). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h)
- **Electronic / Dance / Techno / Club**: Apple Electronic: +4.3/+3.8 (32/64), +1.2, 0, **−2.2 (500)**, +2.3 (1k), +0.9, +1.3, +4 (8k), **+4.8 (16k)** (V with notch at 500). Apple Dance: +3.6 (32), **+6.6 (64)**, +5 (125), 0 (250), +1.9, +3.7, +5.2 (2k), +4.5, +3.6, 0 (16k) — the largest Apple boost after R&B. Winamp Dance: ≈ +6/+4 lows, then −4…−5 from 3k–12k, flat 14–16k (bass up, treble down). Winamp Techno: V ≈ +5 (60), −4 (600), +5…+6 (6–16k). Winamp Club: mid lift only (+2…+3 at 310 Hz–3k). AOSP Dance: +6, 0, +2, +4, +1. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h)
- **Hip-Hop / R&B / Deep**: Apple Hip-Hop: +5 (32), +4.3 (64), +1.5, +3 (250), −1 (500/1k), +1.5, −0.5, +2, +3. Apple R&B: +2.6 (32), **+6.9 (64)**, +5.7 (125), +1.3, −2.2 (500), −1.5, +2.3, +2.7, +3, +3.8 — highest boost in the Apple-family set. Apple Deep: bass up (+5 at 32) and treble down (−2.2, −3.6, −4.6 at 4k/8k/16k), small mid lift (~+2.5–2.9 at 500/1k). AOSP Hip Hop: +5, +3, 0, +1, +3. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [AOSP](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h)
- **Headphones / "Laptop speakers/headphones"** (Winamp/VLC): ≈ +3 (60), **+7 (170)**, +3 (310), −3 (600), −2 (1k), +1 (3k), +3 (6k), +6 (12k), +8 (14k), **+9 (16k)** (approx.); VLC version reaches +14.4 dB at 16k. Shape = mid-bass bump + strong top-octave lift + lower-mid dip. No Apple equivalent. — [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); [VLC](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h)
- **Acoustic**: Apple family (Cog): +5 (32), +4.9 (64), +4 (125), +1.1 (250), +2.2 (500), +1.8 (1k), +3.5 (2k), +4.1 (4k), +3.6 (8k), +2.2 (16k) — broad mild lift, lowest at 250 Hz. eqMac's Acoustic is an outlier with a different shape (−8.3, +9.8, −15.7, …, +18.2 dB), i.e. far outside ±12 and not a 2× scaling — likely a data error. — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- Other Apple-family shapes (brief): **Jazz** smile like Classical (+4 low, −1.5 at 500/1k, +3.8 at 16k); **Latin** smile with flat 125–250, −1.5 at 500–2k, +4.5 at both ends; **Piano** broad lift (+2…+4.5, dip 0 at 125); **Lounge** odd: −3 (32), +4 (500), −2.5 (1k), +2 (8k) — eqMac has +5 at 1k (sign disagreement with Cog). — [Cog](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [eqMac](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)
- Other Winamp shapes (brief, approx.): **Large Hall** +6 lows, −3 at 3–12k; **Live** −3 at 60, +2…+3 mids, +1…+2 top; **Reggae** −4 at 600, +4 at 3–6k; **Ska** low cut (−2…−3) rising to +6…+7 top; **Soft** +3 at 60, slight dip, +5…+7 top; **Soft Rock** +2 lows, −3…−4 at 1–3k, +5 at 16k. — [Webamp](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json)
- AOSP ships two tables: "Normal" (aggressive, up to +15 dB, e.g. Dance 15/−6/7/13/10) and "Soft" (used at runtime, max +9 for Heavy Metal at 910 Hz). The code applies the Soft table (`EQNB_5BandSoftPresets`) when a preset is selected. — [AOSP EffectBundle.h](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.h); [AOSP EffectBundle.cpp](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/heads/main/media/libeffects/lvm/wrapper/Bundle/EffectBundle.cpp)

### Inferences
- Agreement: Bass/Treble Booster/Reducer and Small Speakers are smooth monotonic ramps reaching ≈ ±5.5 dB at the extreme band, falling to 0 at the 1 kHz pivot (Apple family, consistent across Cog/eqMac shapes). Pop = mid hump +4–5 dB. Rock/Electronic/Hip-Hop/Jazz/Latin = smile/V with 1–5 dB mid dip. Spoken Word/Vocal = 500 Hz–4 kHz lift +4–5 dB with low cut.
- Disagreement: Classical (Apple smile vs Winamp treble cut), Dance (Apple mid/upper lift vs Winamp treble cut), Lounge, Acoustic; and overall magnitude (Apple ~+5 vs Winamp ~+7…+10 vs VLC/eqMac up to +11…+18).
- Apple-family values look like a "×0.5 of eqMac" or eqMac is "×2 of Apple"; which is original I could not determine. The Cog set matches the commonly circulated iTunes values in shape, but **I did not verify the Cog file against Apple's own preset data** (Cog does not state provenance in the code I read).
- For a ±12 dB, 10-band target, Apple-family magnitudes (max ≈ +5.5…+7 dB) fit comfortably; Winamp/VLC magnitudes would need halving to stay musical.

### Gaps
- Apple Late Night: no reliable band values or behaviour description found.
- Exact Winamp dB mapping (1–64 slider) is approximated; Webamp's ±12 dB assumption was used.
- Samsung, Poweramp, Spotify (desktop/iOS) preset values not obtained.

## Q3. Do apps apply pre-amp / headroom automatically?

### Takeaway
Most store a preamp field but **do not auto-compensate** in the preset itself: Apple-family presets (Cog file) all store preamp 0 dB; Winamp presets all store preamp at the 0 dB position; VLC stores per-preset positive preamp values (3–12) whose semantics I did not verify. Peace/APO offer explicit or optional anti-clipping preamp reduction. Standard advice: set preamp ≈ −(largest boost).

### Cited Findings
- Apple iTunes/Music EQ has a Preamp slider (far left) controlling "the overall volume of frequencies" — [Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/itunes/itns2991/windows)
- All 22 Apple-family presets in Cog.q1.json have preamp = 201 → 0 dB, i.e. no headroom built in — [Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json), scale per [Cog Equalizer.swift](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/iOS/App/Equalizer.swift)
- All Webamp/Winamp built-in presets have preamp = 33 (≈ 0 dB on the 1–64 scale) — [Webamp builtin.json](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json); Webamp preamp 0–100 maps to −12…+12 dB, 50 = 0 dB — [Webamp media/index.ts](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/js/media/index.ts)
- VLC stores a preamp per preset: flat 12, classical 12, club/party/pop/ska 6, live/softrock 7, reggae 8, dance/fullbass/largehall/rock/soft/techno 5, headphones/fullbasstreble 4, fulltreble 3 — i.e. the more a preset boosts, the lower its preamp (relative headroom ≈ 7–9 dB vs flat) — [VLC equalizer_presets.h](https://github.com/videolan/vlc/blob/master/modules/audio_filter/equalizer_presets.h). Whether VLC's absolute preamp of 12 for flat means +12 dB gain or is an offset was **not verified**.
- eqMac's preset struct has a `global` gain field alongside `bands` — [eqMac source](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift); whether defaults set it negative was not checked.

## Q4. Criticism of generic genre presets; which presets users find useful

### Takeaway
Engineers and audiophile forums argue genre presets are crude because mixes are made flat and genre doesn't predict a track's spectrum; EQ is better used to correct the playback system (headphone/speaker), with cuts preferred over boosts. Use-case presets (bass/treble tilt, vocal/speech, small speakers) and device-correction curves are the commonly recommended uses. Evidence is mostly forum opinion.

### Cited Findings
- A mix is made on flat monitors; applying a fixed curve distorts the intent; mastering engineer Steve Hoffman is cited saying a flat master lets listeners set their own EQ — [Wikipedia: Smiley face curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiley_face_curve), [Wikipedia: Remaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remaster), [Head-Fi: smiley curve](https://www.head-fi.org/threads/smiley-curve-normal.514007/) (snippets)
- Counterpoint: the smiley curve is not inherently bad; the issue is applying it to everything; EQ is mainly for system correction and cuts work better than boosts — [KVR forum](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=455662), [AudioKarma](https://audiokarma.org/forums/threads/disco-smile-eq-settings-hmmm.337002/) (snippets)
- Headphone EQ community practice favours per-model correction (e.g. AutoEQ profiles distributed as iTunes 10-band presets with a large negative preamp, e.g. −13 dB for one HD595 profile) — [gist using AutoEQ for iTunes EQ](https://gist.github.com/joneskoo/115f37d8afc84d490f753bec184d4837)
- Recent open-source EQ apps ship use-case presets (Bass Boost, Treble Boost, Vocal Clarity, Loudness, Podcast) alongside genre ones, described as "low-end boost, everything else flat", "lows cut, upper mids boosted", "rumble cut and speech range boosted" — [iQualize presets wiki](https://github.com/dariuscorvus/iqualize/wiki/Presets)

### Inferences
- Presets most defensible for a gaming/headphone DAC: Flat, Bass boost, Bass cut, Treble boost/cut, Loudness (quiet listening), Vocal/Speech, Small speakers/Laptop, Headphones-style V. Genre presets are expected by users but are the least principled.

### Gaps
- No quantitative user-preference data (e.g. which presets are most selected) found.
