# Retune ten G6 presets for real headroom

The best built-in set for the G6 keeps the shape families the app already ships, but changes most of the values. Seven of the ten are revised, Vocal is replaced, and every pre-amp is sized to the summed curve rather than to the tallest slider. The recommended final set is **Flat, Bass boost, Bass cut, Treble boost, Warm, V-shape, Loudness, Speech, Footsteps and Small speakers**, with all boosts kept at Apple-family scale (largest slider no higher than +5 dB) so that the G6's −6 dB pre-amp floor can cover each curve. This matters because, under a standard peaking-filter model, **every boosted placeholder preset currently peaks about 0.6–1.2 dB above its own pre-amp**. The current Bass boost (6,5,4,2) sums to about +7.1 dB against a −6 dB pre-amp, and to +8.3 dB if the G6 bands are wider. Only two parts of the set have solid evidence behind them. The bass shelf comes from Harman preference research, and the Loudness and Speech weightings come from ISO 226 and ANSI S3.5 (the SII standard). Footsteps, V-shape, Treble boost and the exact speaker values rest on convention or vendor and folk advice, and the tables below label them that way. One caveat covers everything: Creative does not document the G6's filter type, Q or internal DSP headroom. All the summation numbers below come from a model and need checking on hardware, using the measurement plan at the end.

## Every boosted placeholder preset is under-protected by about one decibel

On an octave graphic EQ, neighbouring bands overlap, so the combined curve rises above the largest single slider. Rane documents two adjacent +6 dB proportional-Q sliders producing about +9 dB, and about +7 dB with constant-Q designs ([RaneNote 154](https://ranecommercial.com/legacy/note154.html); [Rane Constant-Q](https://www.ranecommercial.com/legacy/pdf/ranenotes/Constant_Q_Graphic_Equalizers.pdf)). The usual anti-clipping rule from AutoEq and Equalizer APO practice is to set a negative pre-amp equal to the **peak of the combined curve**, plus a little more where filters overlap ([AutoEq README](https://github.com/iwalton3/AutoEq); [Head-Fi APO thread](https://www.head-fi.org/threads/equalizer-apo-questions-discussion.953410/post-16135678)). The model below treats each band as an RBJ peaking biquad at 48 kHz, with peaks measured on a dense 20 Hz–20 kHz grid. Q 1.41 is the mathematical one-octave bandwidth, Q 1.0 is somewhat wider, and Q 0.7 approximates proportional-Q "wide" bands. These are my computations from that model, not G6 measurements.

The shortfalls are small, but they add to real programme peaks. Across more than 107,000 tracks, Archimago found that covering 99% of true peaks needs **+2.35 dB of headroom for rock, +2.87 dB for pop and +4.22 dB for electronica**. He saw true peaks as high as +7.72 dBTP and recommends about 3 dB of DAC headroom ([Archimago 2025](https://archimago.blogspot.com/2025/08/a-look-at-music-library-how-much.html?m=1)). If the G6 DSP has no internal guard bits, a preset that is 1 dB under-protected on a loud master clips audibly. If it does have them, the risk is smaller. That is unknown, which is why the recommendations below keep a margin.

The Scout row has a practical consequence. The project verified that Scout Mode writes its own curve but leaves the pre-amp alone, and the app keeps the pre-amp editable while Scout is on (`docs/protocol-g6.md`, `app/API.md`). Scout's curve peaks at about +5 dB around 4 kHz in the model. The app should therefore suggest **pre-amp −5 to −6 dB while Scout is active**, which Sound Blaster Command cannot do because it locks the pre-amp.

## Ten presets, sized so the −6 dB floor always suffices

The final set uses two design rules. First, the largest slider stays at or below +5 dB, and the combined peak stays at or below about +6 dB even with Q 1.0 bands. Second, the pre-amp is set to **−ceil₀.₅(peak at Q 1.0)**, capped at −6. That covers the one-octave model with 0.3–0.7 dB of spare margin and also covers a moderately wider filter. Cut-only presets get a pre-amp of 0, because a positive pre-amp would add to inter-sample peaks. ### The bass shelf is the one curve with strong preference data

Measured against a diffuse-field-neutral headphone, the Harman over-ear preference amounts to a low shelf of roughly +4.3 to +6.4 dB below about 105 Hz plus a small treble trim. RTINGS uses a 4.3 dB shelf at 105 Hz with Q 0.707 ([RTINGS changelog](https://www.rtings.com/headphones/tests/changelogs/1-8)). Olive's 2024 pilot target for the B&K 5128 used **+6.4 dB below 105 Hz and −1.4 dB above 2.5 kHz** ([SoundGuys](https://www.soundguys.com/harman-research-targets-the-bruel-kjaer-5128-112247)). In Olive's segmentation study, **64% of listeners preferred the Harman target and about 21% wanted less bass** ([Acoustics Today](https://acousticstoday.org/issues/2022AT/Spring2022/files/basic-html/page66.html)). Preferred bass in Olive's 2025 adjustment study ranged from about +2 to +9 dB ([AES e-library 22928](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=22928)). The revised Bass boost (5, 4, 1.5) produces a combined response of **+5.8, +5.2, +2.4, +0.5 dB** at 31, 62, 125 and 250 Hz in the model. That is a close match to a 6 dB, 105 Hz shelf (computed: +6.0, +5.3, +2.0, +0.2), and it stays inside the pre-amp floor. The current 6,5,4,2 is a broad ramp that reaches into 250 Hz "mud" territory and overshoots. I left the −1.4 dB treble trim out on purpose, so that "Bass boost" does what its name says. A separate "Harman-style" variant could add −1 at 4k and −1.5 at 8k and 16k.

Bass cut already mirrors Apple's Bass Reducer, which runs from −5.5 dB at 32 Hz tapering to 0 by 1 kHz ([Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json)). It needs no headroom, so it stays unchanged.

### Loudness should follow ISO 226, not a smile

The placeholder Loudness preset boosts 8k and 16k and dips 500 Hz. ISO 226 asks for something else. Using the 2003 formula, dropping from 80 to 60 phon calls for about **+9.4 dB at 31 Hz, +5.5 at 125, +3.2 at 250, essentially 0 from 1 to 8 kHz, and +3.2 at 12.5 kHz** relative to 1 kHz ([ISO 226 code](https://dsprelated.com/showcode/174.php); [Forum Acusticum 2023](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2023/data/articles/000585.pdf)). Full compensation exceeds what the pre-amp can protect. Pure-tone contours also overstate what music needs. The revised curve therefore delivers about 55–65% of the 80→60 phon correction: the modelled result is +5.2, +4.9, +3.6, +2.2 and +0.9 dB from 31 to 500 Hz, flat through 8 kHz, and +2 dB at 16 kHz. The 16 kHz value is an extrapolation, because ISO data stop at 12.5 kHz. Apple's own Loudness preset has a −5 dB dip at 4 kHz next to a +5 dB bump at 8 kHz ([Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json)), which does not follow the contours, so convention gives no reason to copy it. For quiet listening late at night, static EQ is the weaker tool. Dynamic-range control works better, as Dolby's Line and RF DRC modes and AVR Night modes show ([Microsoft AC-3 decoder API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/codecapi/ne-codecapi-eavdecddoperationalmode)). The G6's Smart Volume "night" mode (feature `96:06` = 2 in `docs/protocol-g6.md`) should be suggested alongside this preset.

### Speech follows the SII band ranking

In the octave-band procedure of ANSI S3.5, the importance weights are about **0.26 at 2 kHz, 0.24 at 1 kHz, 0.21 at 4 kHz, 0.17 at 500 Hz and only 0.06 at both 250 Hz and 8 kHz** ([R SII package](https://rdrr.io/cran/SII/man/sii.html); [ANSI blog](https://blog.ansi.org/speech-intelligibility-index)). The revised Speech preset puts its largest lift at 2k (+2.5), smaller lifts at 4k (+2) and 1k (+1), and moves the placeholder's +2 at 500 Hz to 0, because 500 Hz is where "boxiness" lives ([iZotope cheat sheet](https://www.izotope.com/en/learn/eq-cheat-sheet)). The 31 and 62 Hz cuts stand in for the 80–100 Hz high-pass that podcast engineers use ([Cleanvoice](https://cleanvoice.ai/blog/podcast-eq/)), because the G6 graphic EQ has no high-pass filter. The −1.5 at 250 is the de-mud move. Playback EQ cannot raise intelligibility for speech that is already fully audible. Its value lies in reducing low-frequency masking and compensating for headphones with recessed upper mids. Because the curve is cut-heavy, the pre-amp only needs to be −3.5.

**Vocal is replaced, not kept.** Its shape (lows cut, a 500 Hz–4 kHz hump) is essentially Speech with less low cut, and it duplicates Apple's Vocal Booster and Spoken Word pair ([Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json)). Users who want vocals forward in music get most of that from Speech. The freed slot is more valuable for desktop speakers.

### Small speakers cuts what the drivers cannot play

Apple's Small Speakers preset boosts 32 Hz by about +5.5 dB ([Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json)). That is backwards for real small drivers. Boosting below a speaker's cutoff drives the cone past its linear range and adds distortion ([US Patent 9344822](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9344822)). Below the port tuning frequency, the excursion-limited output collapses ([Cordell EQSS](https://www.cordellaudio.com/loudspeakers/EQSS_White_Paper.pdf)). Decent 4-inch desktop monitors measure about **F3 ≈ 60 Hz (Edifier MR4.5)** or reach down to about 45 Hz (Topping MA4) ([Erin's Audio Corner MR4.5](https://erinsaudiocorner.com/loudspeakers/edifier_mr4_5/); [Erin's Audio Corner MA4](https://erinsaudiocorner.com/loudspeakers/topping_ma4/)). The recommended curve cuts 31 Hz by 6 dB and 62 Hz by 2 dB, adds +1.5 dB at 125 and 250 Hz where these speakers still have output, and adds a small presence lift. It is a middle ground between the notes' laptop curve (−12 at 31) and their 4-inch-monitor curve, and it is aimed at the G6's speaker output rather than at laptops.

### Warm, Treble boost and V-shape follow preference tilt and convention

Olive found that preferred room corrections follow "a smooth downward slope with increasing frequency" ([Olive blog](https://seanolive.blogspot.ca/2009/11/subjective-and-objective-evaluation-of.html)). Secondary summaries put the preferred slope at about −0.5 to −1 dB per octave ([Archimago 2026](https://archimago.blogspot.com/2026/07/room-target-curves-then-now-and-options.html)). Harshness tends to sit at 5–9 kHz, where sealed-ear resonances and sibilance fall ([US Patent 9282397](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/9282397); [theaudiostuff glossary](https://theaudiostuff.com/glossary/sibilance/)). The revised Warm preset is a gentle tilt of about −0.6 dB per octave, running from about +2.3 dB at 31 Hz to −3.2 dB at 16 kHz in the model. Its broad cuts fall at 4–16 kHz, and it trims the placeholder's +3 dB bass, which summed to +4 dB.

Treble boost and V-shape have no preference research that supports them as targets. They stay because users expect them: Apple's Treble Booster ramps to +5.5 dB at 16 kHz, and smile curves recur in Rock, Electronic and Winamp's Party ([Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json); [Webamp builtin.json](https://github.com/captbaritone/webamp/blob/master/packages/webamp/presets/builtin.json)). Both are scaled down. Treble boost reaches +2 dB at 4 kHz instead of +4, because headphones already carry about +8 dB of ear gain at 3–4 kHz in the Harman eardrum target ([AutoEq Harman over-ear CSV](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jaakkopasanen/AutoEq/master/targets/Harman%20over-ear%202018.csv)), so presence boosts turn harsh quickly. V-shape loses about 1 dB at each end so that its summed peak fits inside a −5.5 dB pre-amp.

### Footsteps is the least-evidenced preset and should look it

Every footstep EQ source I found is vendor or folk advice. SteelSeries places footsteps at "around 1 kHz to 4 kHz" and gives no dB values ([SteelSeries](https://steelseries.com/blog/best-eq-settings-for-gaming)). Other guides suggest about +4 dB near 3 kHz and warn that boosting all of 2–4 kHz makes gunfire harsh ([Attack Shark](https://attackshark.com/blogs/knowledges/fps-footstep-frequencies-2khz-4khz-mastery)). Riot's only on-record statement concerns attenuation curves, not spectra ([Riot, Ask VALORANT #6](https://riot.com/31zfCBO)). Creative describes Scout Mode only qualitatively ([mundobytes explainer](https://mundobytes.com/en/scout-mode-in-creative-app)). The revised Footsteps preset is +2 dB at 2k, +3.5 dB at 4k and +2 dB at 8k, with the lows trimmed by 3 dB at 31 Hz and 2.5 dB at 62 Hz to reduce masking from explosions. That lowers the placeholder's summed peak from +6.2 to +4.1 dB. Sources disagree about cutting bass. One CS2 guide argues the game's bass is intentional and should be kept ([Switchblade Gaming](https://www.switchbladegaming.com/game-settings/best-gaming-headset/)), and Scout itself keeps +2 to +3 dB from 31 to 125 Hz. A subtractive approach is more defensible: Steam users suggest cutting the loud gunfire range and raising volume instead ([Steam CS2 discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/730/discussions/0/3881597531956789677)). Scout's real advantage is probably its Smart Volume compression rather than its EQ, so a static Footsteps preset should not be marketed as equivalent to Scout.

## Building shelves and tilts from octave sliders

A one-octave graphic band (Q ≈ 1.41) spills about half its gain half an octave away and about 18% one octave away. In the model, a single +6 dB band gives +3.0 dB at ±½ octave and +1.1 dB at ±1 octave. A plateau therefore needs each slider set about **1 dB below the target level** when its neighbours are boosted similarly, and less than that if the bands are wider. Ramps should step no more than 2–3 dB per octave to avoid ripple. Symetrix documents the trade-off: narrow bands leave about 1 dB of ripple between adjacent boosts, and wide bands add more total gain ([Symetrix docs](https://composer-docs.symetrix.co/software/toolkit/design-view/eqs/Graphic_EQ.html)).

The most important practical rule is to fit the *combined* response, not the slider values. A least-squares fit of a 6 dB, 105 Hz low shelf gives 5.5, 4.5, 1.5, −0.5. A naive 6, 6, 4, 2 overshoots to +7.9 dB. No feature narrower than one octave is possible, so a graphic EQ cannot notch a specific headphone's 6 kHz peak. The 16 kHz band also sits close to Nyquist at 44.1 and 48 kHz, so its upper skirt is cramped and its effect above about 16 kHz is uncertain.

For headroom, the order of preference is to cut rather than boost, to express "more X" partly as "less of everything else", and to size the pre-amp from the summed peak. Because the G6 pre-amp stops at −6 dB, any preset whose summed peak exceeds about +6 dB cannot be fully protected. That is the hard reason to stay at Apple-family magnitudes (peaks around +4 to +7 dB) rather than Winamp or eqMac scale. eqMac doubles Apple's values, so its Bass Booster reaches +11 dB ([eqMac presets](https://github.com/bitgapp/eqMac/blob/master/native/app/Source/Audio/Effects/Equalizers/Advanced/AdvancedEqualizerDefaultPresets.swift)). Apple-family presets also store a pre-amp of 0 ([Cog.q1.json](https://github.com/losnoco/Cog/blob/main/Cog.q1.json)), so the app's habit of shipping a negative pre-amp is already better than common practice. The negative pre-amp makes presets quieter, though, and users will compare them against Flat at a lower level and judge them "worse" ([SoundGuys](https://www.soundguys.com/headphone-eq-settings-explained-159075/)). The UI should say that.

## What generic presets cannot know, and how to measure the G6

A generic preset applies a *delta* to an unknown transducer. A bass shelf that is right for a diffuse-field-tuned headphone over-boosts one that already has a Harman-like lift. Individual taste also spans about ±3 dB in bass and treble ([AES e-library 22928](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=22928)). Engineers' standard criticism is that mixes are made flat, so a fixed genre curve distorts intent, and EQ earns its keep mainly as correction for a specific headphone or speaker ([Wikipedia: Smiley face curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiley_face_curve); [KVR forum](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?t=455662)). That is why this set has use-case presets rather than genre presets. It is also why the larger long-term gain would come from importing per-model AutoEq corrections, which already ship as 10-band iTunes-style presets with large negative pre-amps ([AutoEQ to iTunes gist](https://gist.github.com/joneskoo/115f37d8afc84d490f753bec184d4837)). That use depends on knowing the G6 band shapes.

Those shapes are undocumented, and so is the internal headroom. Four measurements would settle them. Use any audio interface to capture the G6 line or headphone output at low analogue volume (so the analogue stage does not clip first), and use REW or a similar tool with sweeps:

1. **Band shape and Q.** Set one band to +12 dB with everything else at 0, sweep, read the −3 dB bandwidth, and repeat at +6 dB to see whether Q is constant or proportional.
2. **Summation.** Set two adjacent bands, and then three, to +6 dB. Compare the measured peak with the +7.1 and +8.3 dB predicted at Q 1.41. If the result is higher, raise the pre-amps in the table, or lower the sliders.
3. **Internal headroom.** Play a 0 dBFS sine at 62 Hz with the 62 Hz band at +6 and pre-amp 0, then again with pre-amp −6, and look for clipping harmonics in the first capture. Clean output in the first case means floating-point or guard-bit headroom exists, and the margins in this report become conservative.
4. **Order of operations.** Repeat test 3 at +12 dB to see whether clipping happens before or after the pre-amp, which shows whether the pre-amp precedes the EQ.

Testing at 44.1 and 48 kHz would also show how the 16 kHz band behaves near Nyquist.

Two implementation notes come from the repo. Built-in names are duplicated in `app/ui/src/lib/eqCurves.ts` and `app/src-tauri/src/eq_curves.rs` (`RESERVED_NAMES`), so renaming Vocal to Small speakers must update both. A user who already saved a curve named "Small speakers" would collide with the new reserved name, so that case needs a migration rule. Chips match on exact values, so curves applied under the old values will now show as "Custom".

## Conclusion

The most consequential fix is arithmetic, not taste. The placeholder shapes were mostly reasonable, but they were sized slider by slider, and on overlapping octave bands that leaves every boosted preset short of headroom on a device whose pre-amp cannot go below −6 dB. Once presets are sized to the summed curve, the evidence base turns out to be uneven. Harman research supports the bass shelf, ISO 226 supports the Loudness shape and the SII supports the Speech weighting. Everything else, Footsteps especially, is convention dressed as science, and it should be kept gentle.

The G6 measurement is worth doing before the next release. It turns the pre-amp values from modelled to verified, and it is the prerequisite for the one upgrade generic presets can never match: per-headphone correction curves fitted to the G6's real band shapes.
